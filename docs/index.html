Nowcast: method, data and limits
Method — a PCE component whose BLS source series is out is translated into its PCE counterpart (blends of several series are fitted to BEA’s own history; seasonal adjustment is redone with X-13 where BEA does it itself). A component not yet released is forecast by the average of its 3-month and 12-month mean change; gasoline comes from weekly retail prices. Components are weighted by last month’s spending shares. CPI is built the same way from 71 items weighted by BLS relative importance.
What to expect — before CPI is released, accuracy is about the same as a simple regression baseline (the “DMS baseline” in the table, a simplified version of the Knotek–Zaman model): the error is mostly noise in individual components that no available indicator predicts. The gain comes after CPI and PPI arrive. BEA’s first print differs from its later revised value by about 5 basis points on average for core PCE, which no model can recover.
Backtest — partly real-time: targets are BEA’s first prints, but BEA’s component history and spending shares are at today’s vintage (BEA’s detail tables are not archived) and seasonal factors use the full sample. The Cleveland Fed figures are genuinely real-time. A true real-time record builds from the first daily run. Months with missing data (for example no October 2025 CPI) are filled from earlier data.

Core CPI Inflation: Persistence and Expectations

Two independent models of core CPI inflation, updated as new data arrives.

The models ask how much inflation’s past behavior and households’ inflation expectations tell us about where inflation is headed. Persistence model — a Bayesian Phillips curve combining a prior estimated from 1958–99 inflation dynamics with 2000–present data; a heavier weight on the prior anchors the forecast toward that pre‑2000 relationship, a lighter weight lets recent data dominate. Expectations model — a Phillips curve using University of Michigan survey expectations in place of (pure) or alongside (hybrid) lagged inflation.

Persistence model based on M.T. Kiley (2022), “Anchored or Not: How Much Information Does 21st Century Data Contain on Inflation Dynamics?,” FEDS Working Paper 2022‑016, and M.T. Kiley (2023), “A (Bayesian) Update on Inflation and Inflation Persistence,” FEDS Notes.

Expectations model based on M.T. Kiley (2015), “Low Inflation in the United States: A Summary of Recent Research,” FEDS Notes, and J.M. Roberts (1995), “New Keynesian Economics and the Phillips Curve,” Journal of Money, Credit and Banking, 27(4), 975–984.
